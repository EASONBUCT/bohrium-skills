# Page structure, density, and phrasing

## One page = one claim

Every slide carries exactly one claim (the slide title states it). Supporting points are bullets that answer "how do we know this claim?" — not a new claim.

## Bullet phrasing

- 3–5 bullets per content slide; 6 is the hard ceiling before the page overflows.
- Phrase bullets as short noun/verb groups ("MD 预测 12.3→8.1 W/m·K"), not full sentences.
- No bullet may introduce a number, unit, method name, or conclusion that differs from the paper. Transcribe verbatim; do not re-round.

## Layout selection

| layout | use |
|---|---|
| title | opening title page (paper title, authors, venue, date) |
| section | transition/agenda pages between result blocks |
| bullets | a claim supported by prose or a small table |
| bullets_figure | a claim whose primary evidence is one figure |
| figure | one figure with a one-line claim in the title |
| table | a claim whose evidence is a table |

## Overflow and density rules

- Title ≤ 2 lines at 24–28 pt in the template; if longer, shorten the claim, never shrink below 18 pt.
- Body ≤ 6 bullets; if a bullet wraps to >2 lines, split or shorten it.
- A figure page shows at most 2 figures side by side; keep the caption text separate from the title.
- Tables wider than 6 columns go to a table layout and use the template's smallest legible font (≥ 12 pt).

## Information hierarchy

- Mainline claims (problem / approach / key result / conclusion / limitation) are mandatory pages; supporting detail is cuttable.
- When the slide-count cap forces compression, merge support pages first; keep the conclusion page.
- Never merge two unrelated claims into one page to save slides; drop the weakest support page instead and record the cut in `change_log`.
